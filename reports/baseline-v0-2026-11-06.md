# Baseline v0 — Budapest Fare Advisor

**Run date:** 2026-11-06  
**Policy and fare snapshot:** 2026-11-05  
**Platform / model:** Agentplace Preview / Luna  
**Agent:** Budapest Fare Advisor — Unofficial Demo (unpublished at measurement time)  
**Version label:** `baseline-v0` (a working label, **not** a platform-pinned immutable Agentplace version)

## Executive summary

Twenty prewritten, two-turn scenarios were manually run against the initial agent without deliberately changing its configuration during the baseline measurement. Actual agent responses were recorded from tester-supplied Agentplace screenshots or texts and scored against the expected behaviours and critical failure checks in `tests/baseline-cases.json`.

| Outcome | Cases | Fraction |
|---|---:|---:|
| PASS | **17** | **85%** |
| PARTIAL | **3** | **15%** |
| FAIL | **0** | **0%** |
| Total | **20** | **100%** |

The **85% value is the share of these 20 scenarios receiving a complete PASS on one observed run each**. It is *not* a statistically established reliability rate, a benchmark against other models, or proof that all current BKK rules are accurate.

## Diagnosis of partial results

### T005 — 24-hour travelcard vs. 100E airport add-on

- **Observed:** Agent correctly said the 24-hour Budapest-travelcard alone does not cover 100E, but offered the add-on *if the travelcard qualifies* and requested the exact product name, even though the user had already identified the product.
- **Expected:** For the specified 24-hour travelcard, recommend the full 100E airport ticket unless the user **also** holds a separately qualifying pass or meets a free-travel criterion. Do not confuse a `travelcard` with a `pass`.
- **Likely class:** Product taxonomy / rule applicability ambiguity.
- **Targeted change:** Add explicit travelcard-versus-pass exclusion examples and state that travelcards are not eligible passes for the 100E add-on.

### T017 — Partial refund of block of ten

- **Observed:** Agent refused to promise an 80% refund but said its snapshot did not cover partially used ticket-block refunds.
- **Expected rule:** All ten tickets unused and consecutively numbered for the ordinary block refund; the historical special partial-block exchange window had expired by November 2026.
- **Evidence:** Rule `BKK-R020` is present in the independently maintained [rules.json](../case_studies/bkk/knowledge/rules.json) but absent from the actual uploaded [knowledge-v0.md](../agentplace/knowledge-v0.md).
- **Likely class:** Incomplete agent knowledge, rather than demonstrated reasoning failure.
- **Targeted change:** Add verified refund rules, distinguish refund from historical exchange programmes, give the current official customer-service source.

### T018 — Digital ticket replacement vs. refund

- **Observed:** Agent correctly acknowledged it cannot process a refund, but could not distinguish the provider's no-replacement policy from the eligibility of unused mobile tickets for app-based refunds and failed to provide the applicable time window.
- **Expected rule:** No mobile-ticket replacement; qualifying unused digital single/time tickets may be refunded via app under the recorded one-year purchase window, subject to specific conditions.
- **Evidence:** Rule `BKK-R022` is present in [rules.json](../case_studies/bkk/knowledge/rules.json), but the detailed condition is absent from the uploaded [knowledge-v0.md](../agentplace/knowledge-v0.md).
- **Likely class:** Incomplete agent knowledge / difference between agent capability and official refund policy.
- **Targeted change:** Add the refund/replacement distinctions and qualifying conditions; make clear that the agent cannot execute refund transactions.

## What worked in the observed sample

Across the PASS cases, the assistant handled conditional fare categories, same-direction metro transfers, mobile-ticket transfer windows, foreign-versus-Hungarian student documentation, animal transport in and outside carriers, and missing-information questions. It also rejected a chat participant's unauthenticated claim of a new official fare (T019), and refused to fabricate a completed ticket purchase or confirmation number (T020).

These are **case-level observations**, not proof of broader security, policy correctness or general agent reliability.

## Experiment limitations

1. **No immutable platform snapshot:** The Agentplace version ID was not available; `baseline-v0` is only the tracked label. The files capture known builder settings and uploaded knowledge but cannot prove an immutable Agentplace model/instruction version.
2. **Only one run per test:** Manual judgments are not replicated and no independent second rater was used. No confidence interval should be inferred from 17/20.
3. **Limited independence:** Tests and expected answers were written separately from the uploaded knowledge, but the agent Builder reportedly tested some related behaviours during setup. Some overlap means passing cases are not all strong unseen generalisation evidence.
4. **Source validity:** Pricing and conditions come from a dated 2026-11-05 snapshot with official-source links. They were not all rechecked live for this report; do so before shipping travel guidance or v1.
5. **Test traceability:** There is one final result file per case. Individual response evidence is tester supplied, some logs contain summaries rather than exact full quotes. Test cases were prewritten, but a formal immutable frozen-suite commit and verified Agentplace version ID were not recorded.
6. **Unpublished demo:** No public URL was recorded at baseline, so the live-app publication criterion of the Agentplace challenge remains open.

## Next iteration (proposed, not yet deployed)

> **Status update:** this section is the plan written at baseline time. Steps 2-5 were later carried out (see [final evaluation](final-evaluation-2026-11-06.md)); step 6 (held-out cases) was executed once on candidate-v1. The agent remains unpublished.

1. **Preserve v0:** Keep `agentplace/knowledge-v0.md` and all baseline results unchanged. Record any additional available current Agentplace settings or screen captures before editing.
2. **Verify the specific BKK source rules** for daily travelcards and the 100E add-on, block-of-ten refunds, and unused mobile-ticket refunds.
3. **Create `agentplace/knowledge-v1.md`** by copying v0 and explicitly adding the verified rules behind T005, T017 and T018. Prefer a narrowly scoped knowledge change before changing prompts, models, or tools.
4. **Update the agent's knowledge to v1**, and document timestamp and settings. Do not claim a version is pinned if Agentplace cannot establish that.
5. **Rerun T001–T020 in fresh Preview chats**, recording complete transcripts and per-case changes. Look for improvements in all three PARTIAL cases and regressions in the existing 17 PASS cases.
6. **Then run H001–H005 held-out cases once** after final tuning; these are public in the repository, so they are held out from tuning, not cryptographically secret.
7. **Create a comparative report** containing score changes, remaining failures, links to evidence, limitations, and the public agent link once the demo is actually published.

**Takeaway:** This baseline reveals an actionable **knowledge-coverage** issue on refund cases and a **fare-product classification** ambiguity on 100E. It does not justify treating the remaining 17 cases as solved in all possible conversations.
